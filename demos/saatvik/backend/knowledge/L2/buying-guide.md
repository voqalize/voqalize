# Choosing a solar system — on-grid, off-grid, hybrid, panels, mistakes

Sources: https://www.saatvikgroup.com/newsroom/blogs/on-grid-vs-off-grid-solar-systems-which-one-suits-you-best, https://www.saatvikgroup.com/newsroom/blogs/how-to-choose-the-best-solar-panel-for-home-in-india, https://www.saatvikgroup.com/newsroom/blogs/top-5-mistakes-people-make-while-installing-solar-panels-for-home-owners, https://www.saatvikgroup.com/newsroom/blogs/solar-panel-warranty-things-you-should-know-before-solar-panel-installation, https://www.saatvikgroup.com/newsroom/blogs/difference-between-solar-module-and-solar-panel, https://www.saatvikgroup.com/modules, https://www.saatvikgroup.com/epc-solutions/rooftop/residential.

## On-grid, off-grid or hybrid
| | On-grid | Off-grid |
|---|---|---|
| Grid connection | yes | no |
| Batteries | not required | required |
| Net metering | available | not applicable |
| Backup in a power cut | no (unless hybrid) | yes |
| Suits | cities, homes, offices | rural areas, farms, remote sites |
| Cost | relatively lower | higher, because of batteries |
- On-grid: best for a city or town with decent supply where the aim is a lower bill; cheaper, and earns net-metering credit. It is the type that qualifies for PM Surya Ghar.
- Off-grid: for frequent power cuts or no grid; running heavy loads (ACs, geysers) needs a properly sized array and battery.
- Hybrid: net-metering savings plus backup in outages. Saatvik's product for this is the UDAY Plus hybrid inverter with the UDAY Shakti battery (topics `inverters`, `batteries`).
- Switching from on-grid to off-grid later is possible but needs batteries and inverter changes, so plan for long-term needs up front.

## Sizing
- Start from the electricity bill: about 120 units a month per kW; 300–400 units a month usually means 3 kW. Roof: about 80–100 sq ft of shadow-free roof per kW (topic `epc-rooftop-residential`).
- The solar calculator on the site gives a quick estimate; a site survey gives the real one.

## Which panel
- N-TOPCon (TEJ, TEJ PLUS): highest efficiency (up to 23.69%), best in heat and low light, lowest degradation, 30-year performance warranty (topic `modules-ntopcon`).
- Mono PERC (VEGA G2WB): proven and economical, up to 21.48%, 25-year performance warranty (topic `modules-mono-perc`).
- Bifacial (VEGA Plus G2TB, and all N-TOPCon): extra yield from reflected light, best on ground mounts, trackers and raised structures (topic `modules-bifacial`).
- For subsidy, choose ALMM-listed, BIS-certified modules with the manufacturer's warranty certificate.

## Common mistakes (Saatvik's blog)
- Buying low-quality, uncertified panels to save money: lower output, faster degradation, weak warranties.
- Wrong direction or tilt: in India panels should ideally face south, tilted for the city's latitude; skipping the site survey is the usual cause.
- No walkway for cleaning: dust then cuts output with no safe way to clean.
- An unlisted vendor: may not be able to handle net metering, subsidy or after-sales; the system may not qualify for incentives.
- Ignoring net metering and DISCOM approvals: the system works but the bill stays high.

## Warranty: what to check
- Product warranty (materials and manufacturing defects; Saatvik up to 12 years) and performance warranty (minimum output over time; Saatvik 30 years on N-TOPCon, 25 on Mono PERC — one blog says all Saatvik modules carry 30 years, which the product pages contradict for Mono PERC).
- Look at the degradation rate, the manufacturer's reputation and support, and the exclusions.
- Warranties are typically voided by installation by unauthorised persons, natural-calamity damage, theft, failure to register the warranty, and lack of maintenance. Details in topic `installation-and-service`.

## Module or panel?
Saatvik's blog: a solar cell is the smallest unit; a module is cells connected inside a frame, the technical term; "panel" is the everyday term and may mean one or more modules, or the whole visible array. When comparing quotes, ask whether a price is for modules alone or for a full system (inverter, wiring, mounting, installation). The blog's own illustration: a module at ₹20–30 per watt, a full 3 kW rooftop system ₹1.5–2 lakh depending on subsidy and installation; the residential page says ₹1.8–2.2 lakh before incentives. Quote the residential page.
