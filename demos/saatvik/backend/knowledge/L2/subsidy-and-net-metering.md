# PM Surya Ghar subsidy and net metering

Sources: https://www.saatvikgroup.com/epc-solutions/rooftop/residential, https://www.saatvikgroup.com/newsroom/blogs/solar-panel-subsidy-in-india-in-2026-a-complete-guide, https://www.saatvikgroup.com/newsroom/blogs/how-rooftop-solar-pays-for-itself-in-under-5-years, https://www.saatvikgroup.com/newsroom/blogs/maximize-your-solar-benefits-navigate-every-scheme-seamlessly-with-saatvik, https://www.saatvikgroup.com/newsroom/blogs/top-5-mistakes-people-make-while-installing-solar-panels-for-home-owners, https://www.saatvikgroup.com/solar-kits.

## Read this first
Subsidy rules, state top-ups, DISCOM procedures, net-metering rules and eligibility vary by state and change over time. Saatvik's own blog posts carry different and partly outdated figures. The agent states only the central PM Surya Ghar figures below, never a state's figure, and sends the visitor to the National Portal for Rooftop Solar and to Saatvik's team for their own case. Saatvik's blogs also mention an older MNRE 40% / 20% capital-subsidy programme; it is not the current scheme and must not be quoted.

## PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana (central scheme)
- Launched in 2024 to subsidise residential rooftop solar; Saatvik's blog says it aims to give households up to 300 units of free electricity a month.
- Central financial assistance (CFA): ₹30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW, ₹18,000 for the third kW; no additional CFA above 3 kW. Maximum ₹78,000 per household.
- Worked out: 1 kW → ₹30,000; 2 kW → ₹60,000; 3 kW or more → ₹78,000.
- Group housing societies and RWAs: ₹18,000 per kW for common facilities, up to 500 kWp (at 3 kWp per house).
- Saatvik's blog says special-category states and UTs (Uttarakhand, Himachal Pradesh, J&K, Ladakh, the Northeast including Sikkim, Andaman & Nicobar, Lakshadweep) have a 10% higher benchmark (₹33,000 and ₹19,800 per kWp). Mention only if asked, and point to the portal.

## Who qualifies (Saatvik's blog)
- A residential property owner, grid-connected, with net metering approved by the DISCOM, installed by an approved (registered) vendor, using MNRE-compliant components.
- Modules must be ALMM-listed; Saatvik's modules are ALMM-listed and its SuryaConnect kits use DCR modules.
- Not covered: off-grid systems, battery-only systems, commercial and industrial projects (C&I instead gets 40% accelerated depreciation; topic `epc-commercial-industrial`).
- One-time per consumer number.

## Documents
Aadhaar card; bank account details and a cancelled cheque; net-metering approval; recent electricity bill; property ownership proof; installation photographs.

## Steps, in order
- Choose a registered vendor (Saatvik is one).
- Apply on the National Portal for Rooftop Solar before installing.
- Install the system to MNRE guidelines.
- DISCOM inspection and net-meter installation.
- Subsidy credited directly to the bank account, typically 2–4 months after successful installation and inspection.
Saatvik, as a registered vendor, prepares and files the application, coordinates the DISCOM inspection and handles net metering end to end.

## Net metering
A bidirectional meter records the power drawn from the grid and the surplus solar exported. At billing time, exports are credited against imports, so the household pays only for net units. Surplus at weekends or holidays is not wasted. Without net metering, excess generation is lost and the bill barely drops, which is a common mistake. Net metering applies to on-grid and hybrid systems, not off-grid ones.

## Economics, as Saatvik presents them
- Residential page: a typical 3 kW system is roughly ₹1.8–2.2 lakh before incentives; payback about 4 years.
- Payback blog: a 3 kW system at about ₹1,80,000, minus ₹78,000 subsidy = ₹1,02,000 net; about 4.2 units per kW per day ≈ 378 units a month; at ₹8 a unit ≈ ₹36,300 a year; payback ≈ 2.8 years, typically 3–5 years on conservative assumptions.
- These are illustrations. Real numbers depend on the state's tariff, sunlight, roof and usage; offer the solar calculator or a site survey.

## Farmers: PM-KUSUM
PM-KUSUM supports solar for farmers: decentralised plants (Component A), standalone off-grid solar pumps (Component B) and solarising grid-connected pumps and feeders (Component C). Subsidy shares and procedures are set by central and state agencies; farmers apply through their state scheme. Saatvik supplies and installs pumps under state awards (topic `solar-pumps`).
