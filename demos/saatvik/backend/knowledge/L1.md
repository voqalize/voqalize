## For a home, in short
The questions most visitors ask first, answered from Saatvik's own buying guide, product and residential pages — no read needed.
- **Which panel for my home?** Saatvik's guide: N-TOPCon (TEJ, TEJ PLUS) is the highest efficiency, best in heat and low light, lowest degradation, with a 30-year performance warranty; Mono PERC (VEGA G2WB) is proven and economical, with 25 years. For a hot Indian rooftop where space is limited, N-TOPCon gets the most from the roof; Mono PERC suits a tighter budget. The SuryaConnect home kits use DCR N-TOPCon modules. For PM Surya Ghar the modules must be DCR, ALMM-listed and BIS-certified. Pages /modules/n-topcon, /modules/mono-perc.
- **Which inverter for my home?** An Uday single-phase on-grid inverter (1.1–6 kW, /inverters/single-phase) when the grid is reliable and the aim is a lower bill. It is grid-connected, the kind of system PM Surya Ghar subsidises, and it does not run in a power cut. The UDAY Plus hybrid inverter (/inverters/hybrid) with the UDAY Shakti battery when they want backup in power cuts. Three-phase Uday inverters (6–50 kW) are for business and industry. Match the inverter to the system size from the bill.
- **What size?** About 120 units a month per kW; a 300–400 unit bill usually needs about 3 kW; about 80–100 sq ft of shadow-free roof per kW. A bill in rupees alone does not give units: ask for their units, or their rate per unit.
- **Or a complete kit:** SuryaConnect, documented in 3 kW and 5 kW sizes (/solar-kits).

## Solar modules (page /modules)
Every module carries a 12-year product warranty. Performance warranty: 25 years for Mono PERC, 30 years for N-TOPCon.

- **N-TOPCon TEJ PLUS, G12R wafer, bifacial glass-to-glass** — 600–640 Wp, up to 23.69% efficiency, 132 half-cut cells, bifaciality 80 ± 5%, 2382 × 1134 × 30 mm, 33 kg (the /modules comparison table still says 33.5 kg), −0.30 %/°C, 1500 V DC. Year-1 degradation under 1.0%, then under 0.40% a year. Saatvik's highest-output module. Page /modules/bifacial-g12r.
- **N-TOPCon TEJ, M10R wafer, bifacial glass-to-glass** — 560–600 Wp, up to 23.23% efficiency, 144 cells, bifaciality 80 ± 5%, 2278 × 1134 × 30 mm, 32 kg (datasheet), −0.30 %/°C. Page /modules/bifacial-g2g-m10r. (The /modules/n-topcon table and FAQ say 560–585 Wp and up to 22.8%; the product page and datasheet say 560–600 Wp and 23.23%.)
- **Mono PERC VEGA Plus G2TB, bifacial** — 525–550 Wp, up to 21.29%, bifaciality 70 ± 5%, −0.36 %/°C, glass and transparent backsheet, 144 half-cells, 2278 × 1134 × 35 mm, 28 kg, 25-year performance warranty. Page /modules/bifacial-g2tb.
- **Mono PERC VEGA G2WB, monofacial** — up to 21.48%, 144 half-cells, 10 busbars, −0.36 %/°C, 2278 × 1134 × 35 mm, 28 kg. Its datasheet covers 525–555 Wp; the product page gives no wattage, and /modules gives the Mono PERC monofacial range as 380–555 Wp. Year-1 degradation under 2.0%, then under 0.55% a year. Page /modules/mono-perc (a near-copy sits at /modules/mono-facial-g2wb).
- N-TOPCon against Mono PERC, in Saatvik's words: higher efficiency (23.69% vs 21.48%), a better temperature coefficient (−0.30 vs −0.36 %/°C), lower degradation (under 0.40% vs 0.55% a year), a 30-year rather than 25-year performance warranty, and 10–30% more energy from bifacial gain. Page /modules/n-topcon.
- Bifacial panels catch light on both faces; Saatvik says up to 30% more energy from reflected light, best on ground-mounted and tracker systems.
- Cells (/solar-cell-technology): N-type TOPCon, 182.3 × 210 mm, 25% average conversion efficiency, average 9.55 Wp per cell.
- Datasheets for open_document: saatvik-n-topcon-g12r-module-datasheet-600-640wp, saatvik-n-topcon-bifacial-module-datasheet (TEJ M10R; also the /modules/n-topcon download), saatvik-mono-perc-bifacial-g2tb-module-datasheet, saatvik-mono-perc-module-datasheet. ## Solar for homes, businesses and land (EPC)
Overview /epc-solutions: turnkey EPC, "from a single home rooftop to utility-scale ground-mount and floating arrays", built to perform 25+ years; 500+ MW of EPC delivered. Every EPC page's buttons ("Contact us", "Ready to Go Solar?", "Contact Sales") go to the /contact form; no EPC page has a price, a booking form, a survey form, a PDF or a calculator link. On the form the categories are "Solar EPC - Rooftop" (homes, businesses, institutions) and "Solar EPC - Utility Ground Mount"; there is none for floating or carport.
- **Homes** (/epc-solutions/rooftop/residential): 1–10 kW. 1 kW makes about 120 units a month; a typical 3 kW system about 360; a 300–400 unit monthly bill usually needs about 3 kW; allow 80–100 sq ft of shadow-free roof per kW. A typical 3 kW system costs roughly ₹1.8–2.2 lakh before subsidy; PM Surya Ghar up to ₹78,000; most homeowners recover the cost in about 4 years; bill savings up to 90%. Most home systems go up in 2–3 days. Saatvik gives a free site survey, files the PM Surya Ghar application as a registered vendor, and arranges net metering with the DISCOM.
- **Businesses and industry** (/epc-solutions/rooftop/commercial): the overview says 50 kW–5 MW; the page says 50 kW to multi-MW. Power cost cut up to 70%; solar at ₹2.5–3.5 a unit against grid ₹8–11. CAPEX: you own it, 40% accelerated depreciation in year one, payback typically 3–4 years. OPEX/RESCO: an investor owns the plant on your roof and you pay a per-unit tariff below the grid rate, no upfront cost.
- **Schools, hospitals, government, non-profits** (/epc-solutions/rooftop/institutional): energy cost cut up to 50%; OPEX/RESCO for institutions without budget; installation planned around academic calendars and hospital operations.
- **Ground-mounted** (/epc-solutions/ground-mounted): 1 MW to 100 MW+; about 4–5 acres per MW (fixed tilt); trackers add 15–20% generation at more capex. Flagship: 100 MW at Raghanseda Solar Park, Gujarat.
- **Floating** (/epc-solutions/floating-solar): reservoirs, dams, lakes, ponds; 5–10% more yield from water cooling, up to 70% less evaporation under the array. The 100 MW Ramagundam project is cited as a benchmark, not as Saatvik's build.
- **Carports** (/epc-solutions/carport): about 3–3.5 kWp per parking bay; EV charging and battery storage can be built in.

## PM Surya Ghar, in brief
- The central government's rooftop solar scheme for homes (PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana). Saatvik's residential page gives the central support as ₹30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW and ₹18,000 for the third kW, up to ₹78,000 in all. Group housing societies and RWAs: ₹18,000 per kW for common facilities (Saatvik's subsidy blog).
- It is for a residential, grid-connected system with net metering, built with DCR (made-in-India cell) modules by a registered vendor. Saatvik's subsidy blog says it is not for off-grid, battery-only or commercial systems.
- You apply on the National Portal for Rooftop Solar before installing; after the DISCOM inspects and fits the net meter, the subsidy goes to your bank account.
- State top-ups, DISCOM rules, net-metering terms and eligibility vary by state and change. Never quote a state figure; send the visitor to the national portal and to Saatvik's team. Topic `subsidy-and-net-metering`.

## The solar calculator (/solar-calculator)
- Inputs: monthly units from the electricity bill, the state or union territory, and the rate per unit in rupees. It returns a system size in kW, cost, subsidy, cost after subsidy, yearly savings and payback, for a residential rooftop.
- Its assumptions, from the page's own code: system size = monthly units ÷ 30 ÷ the state's daily generation per kW; cost = a state-wise DCR price per kW, tiered by system size; subsidy = ₹30,000 for each of the first two kW and ₹18,000 for the third (₹33,000 / ₹33,000 / ₹19,800 in the hill, north-eastern and island states and union territories). The page says: "Indicative estimate for a residential rooftop, based on PM Surya Ghar norms, state-wise sunlight and DCR panel pricing. Contact our team for a site-specific quote." The subsidy line applies "for Residential only, if not already claimed".
- Offer to open it and let the visitor type their numbers; read back what it shows, never compute your own.
