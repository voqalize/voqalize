## Solar modules (page /modules)
Every module carries a 12-year product warranty. Performance warranty: 25 years for Mono PERC, 30 years for N-TOPCon.

- **N-TOPCon TEJ PLUS, G12R wafer, bifacial glass-to-glass** — 600–640 Wp, up to 23.69% efficiency, 132 half-cut cells, 2382 × 1134 × 30 mm, 33.5 kg, temperature coefficient −0.30 %/°C, 1500 V DC. Year-1 degradation under 1.0%, then under 0.40% a year. Saatvik's highest-output module. Page /modules/bifacial-g12r.
- **N-TOPCon TEJ, M10R wafer, bifacial glass-to-glass** — 560–600 Wp, up to 23.23% efficiency, 144 cells, −0.30 %/°C. Page /modules/bifacial-g2g-m10r. (The /modules/n-topcon comparison table gives this range as 560–585 Wp and up to 22.8%; the product page and its own spec table say 560–600 Wp and 23.23%.)
- **Mono PERC VEGA Plus G2TB, bifacial** — 525–550 Wp, up to 21.29%, bifaciality 70 ± 5%, −0.36 %/°C, glass and transparent backsheet, 2278 × 1134 × 35 mm, 28 kg. Page /modules/bifacial-g2tb.
- **Mono PERC VEGA G2WB, monofacial** — 380–555 Wp, up to 21.48%, 144 half-cells, 10 busbars, 2278 × 1134 × 35 mm, 28 kg. Year-1 degradation under 2.0%, then under 0.55% a year. Page /modules/mono-perc.
- N-TOPCon against Mono PERC, in Saatvik's words: higher efficiency (23.69% vs 21.48%), a better temperature coefficient (−0.30 vs −0.36 %/°C), lower degradation (under 0.40% vs 0.55% a year), a 30-year rather than 25-year performance warranty, and 10–30% more energy from bifacial gain. Page /modules/n-topcon.
- Bifacial panels catch light on both faces; Saatvik says up to 30% more energy from reflected light, best on ground-mounted and tracker systems.
- Cells: N-type TOPCon cells averaging 25% conversion efficiency, made in-house (/solar-cell-technology).
- Full spec tables, datasheet figures and which datasheets are readable: topics `modules-ntopcon`, `modules-mono-perc`, `modules-bifacial`.

## Solar for homes, businesses and land (EPC)
- **Homes** (/epc-solutions/rooftop/residential): 1–10 kW systems. Saatvik's rules of thumb: 1 kW makes about 120 units a month; a 300–400 unit monthly bill usually needs about 3 kW; allow 80–100 sq ft of shadow-free roof per kW. A typical 3 kW system costs roughly ₹1.8–2.2 lakh before subsidy, and most homeowners recover the cost in about 4 years. Most home systems go up in 2–3 days. Saatvik files the PM Surya Ghar application, handles net metering with the DISCOM, and gives a free site survey.
- **Businesses and industry** (/epc-solutions/rooftop/commercial): 50 kW to 5 MW rooftops. CAPEX (you own it, 40% accelerated depreciation, typically a 3–4 year payback) or OPEX/RESCO (an investor owns the plant and you buy power below the grid rate with no upfront cost).
- **Schools, hospitals, government** (/epc-solutions/rooftop/institutional), **ground-mounted plants** of 1 MW to 100 MW+ (/epc-solutions/ground-mounted), **floating solar** on reservoirs (/epc-solutions/floating-solar), **solar carports** with EV charging (/epc-solutions/carport).
- Details: topics `epc-rooftop-residential`, `epc-commercial-industrial`, `epc-utility-floating-carport`.

## PM Surya Ghar, in brief
- The central government's rooftop solar scheme for homes (PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana). Saatvik's residential page gives the central support as ₹30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW and ₹18,000 for the third kW, up to ₹78,000 in all. Group housing societies and RWAs: ₹18,000 per kW for common facilities (Saatvik's subsidy blog).
- It is for a residential, grid-connected system with net metering, built with DCR (made-in-India cell) modules by a registered vendor. Saatvik's subsidy blog says it is not for off-grid, battery-only or commercial systems.
- You apply on the National Portal for Rooftop Solar before installing; after the DISCOM inspects and fits the net meter, the subsidy goes to your bank account.
- State top-ups, DISCOM rules, net-metering terms and eligibility vary by state and change. Never quote a state figure; send the visitor to the national portal and to Saatvik's team. Topic `subsidy-and-net-metering`.

## The solar calculator (/solar-calculator)
- Inputs: monthly units from the electricity bill, the state or union territory, and the rate per unit in rupees. It returns a system size in kW, cost, subsidy, cost after subsidy, yearly savings and payback, for a residential rooftop.
- Its assumptions, from the page's own code: system size = monthly units ÷ 30 ÷ the state's daily generation per kW; cost = a state-wise DCR price per kW, tiered by system size; subsidy = ₹30,000 for each of the first two kW and ₹18,000 for the third (₹33,000 / ₹33,000 / ₹19,800 in the hill, north-eastern and island states and union territories). The page says: "Indicative estimate for a residential rooftop, based on PM Surya Ghar norms, state-wise sunlight and DCR panel pricing. Contact our team for a site-specific quote." The subsidy line applies "for Residential only, if not already claimed".
- Offer to open it and let the visitor type their numbers; read back what it shows, never compute your own.

## Certifications and policy
- Modules certified to IEC 61215 and IEC 61730, BIS (IS 14286), CE; on the government's ALMM list; DCR modules for subsidy schemes. ISO 9001 and ISO 14001. PV Cycle member. Published EPD for the G12R module. Page /company/accreditations.
