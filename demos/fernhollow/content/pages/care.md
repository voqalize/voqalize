These are general guides. Where a product comes with its own care instructions, follow those first.

## Wood

Oak, walnut, ash and other solid woods are living materials that respond to light, heat and moisture.

- Dust regularly with a soft, dry or barely damp cloth, following the grain.
- Wipe spills straight away. Do not leave water, wine or oil standing on the surface.
- Use coasters, placemats and trivets for glasses, hot dishes and anything wet.
- Keep wood away from radiators, heaters and long spells of strong direct sunlight, which can dry, crack or fade it.
- Avoid silicone sprays and harsh cleaners. For oiled or waxed finishes, refresh occasionally with a suitable wood oil or wax.
- Expect natural changes. Wood mellows in colour over time, and small marks become part of its character.

## Linen

- Wash cool to warm on a gentle cycle with a mild detergent, and avoid bleach.
- Do not overload the machine; linen needs room to move.
- Line dry or tumble dry on low, and remove while slightly damp to reduce creasing.
- Embrace the crease, or iron while still damp on a medium heat.
- Linen softens with each wash. Some shrinkage on the first wash is normal for natural linen, so follow the label.

## Boucle

- Vacuum gently and regularly with a soft brush attachment to lift dust from the loops.
- Blot spills immediately with a clean, dry cloth. Do not rub, which can distort the loops.
- If a loop pulls, do not cut it close to the base without care; ease it back in with a blunt needle where possible.
- Keep away from sharp claws, velcro and rough fabrics that may snag.
- For stains, use a professional upholstery cleaner.

## Wool

- Vacuum rugs regularly without a beater bar, or with it raised. New wool rugs shed loose fibres for a while; this settles with time.
- Turn rugs every few months so they wear and fade evenly.
- Blot spills from the outside in with a clean cloth and cold water. Avoid hot water and rubbing.
- Wool cushions and throws can usually be aired outdoors to refresh them. Wash only if the label allows, cool and gently, and dry flat.

## Cotton

- Most cotton textiles can be machine washed. Follow the label for temperature.
- Wash darker colours separately the first few times.
- Tumble dry on low or line dry, and avoid over-drying, which can make cotton harsh.

## Leather

- Dust with a soft, dry cloth and keep out of strong direct sunlight and away from heat sources.
- Blot spills straight away with a clean, dry cloth. Avoid soaking the leather.
- Condition every few months with a product suited to the leather type.
- Never use household cleaners, solvents or polish on leather.
- Natural leather develops a patina with use. Small marks and creases are part of its character.

## Ceramic and stoneware

- Check whether a piece is dishwasher, microwave or oven safe before using it that way. Handmade pieces often prefer a hand wash.
- Avoid sudden temperature changes, such as moving a dish straight from the fridge to a hot oven.
- Unglazed areas can absorb liquid; dry them thoroughly.
- For vases, empty and dry them between uses, and use a bottle brush to clean narrow necks.

## Brass

- Dust with a soft, dry cloth.
- Brass darkens and develops a patina naturally over time. Many people prefer this look.
- To restore shine, use a polish made for brass, following its instructions, and test on a small hidden area first.
- Lacquered brass should only be wiped with a damp cloth and dried; polish can damage the lacquer.

## Cast iron

- Wash by hand in warm water with a brush. Avoid soaking and the dishwasher.
- Dry thoroughly straight away, ideally by warming on the hob for a minute.
- Rub a thin layer of cooking oil over the surface after drying to keep it seasoned.
- If rust appears, scrub it away, dry, oil and reseason.
- Decorative cast iron, such as candle holders, only needs dusting and should be kept dry.
