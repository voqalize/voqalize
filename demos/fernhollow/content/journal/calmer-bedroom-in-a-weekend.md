The bedroom is the room we see least with our eyes open, and so it tends to become the place where everything else ends up. The exercise bike, the laundry that never quite gets folded, the boxes from the move. Yet it is also the room that most affects how we feel the rest of the day. A calm bedroom is not a luxury. It is the quickest improvement most homes can make.

Here is a plan for one weekend. Saturday is for subtracting. Sunday is for adding the few things that make the room soft, dark and quiet.

## Saturday morning: clear the decks

Start by taking out everything that is not about sleeping, dressing or resting. Work-related papers, the laptop, the pile of things to return, the chair that has become a wardrobe. If it does not belong to the bedroom, carry it out of the door. You can decide where it lives later; for now, the aim is to see the room as it actually is.

Then look at surfaces. Nightstands, dressers and windowsills collect objects faster than any other furniture in the house. Clear each one completely and put back only what you use daily or love. A good nightstand holds a lamp, a book and a glass of water. That is all.

## Saturday afternoon: hide what must stay

Some things belong in a bedroom but do not need to be seen: spare blankets, chargers, the books you are halfway through. Give them containers. Baskets under the bed or at the foot of it, a lidded box on a shelf, a drawer you finally empty of things that do not belong.

Finish the day by moving the bed, if you can, so that you can see the door from it without lying directly in line with it. Designers and old wisdom agree on this one: a bed placed with a solid wall behind the headboard and a view of the entrance simply feels more settled.

## Sunday morning: the bed itself

The bed is the room's centre, and the calmest bedrooms let it be simple. A low frame keeps sightlines open and makes the ceiling feel higher.

## Sunday afternoon: light and dark

Sleep likes darkness, and waking likes daylight. Your windows need to do both. The answer is layers: a light-filtering curtain for the day, and a heavier blackout curtain for the night. Hang the rod higher and wider than the window frame, close to the ceiling and a hand's width or more past each side, so the curtains frame the window rather than covering it and let in the most daylight when they are open.

## Small habits that keep it calm

- **One basket for the floordrobe.** Give the clothes that are not dirty but not clean a home, and empty it every few days.
- **No screens on the nightstand.** Charge the phone across the room or outside it.
- **Air the room daily.** Open the window for ten minutes each morning, even in winter.
- **A five-minute reset.** Before bed, return everything that drifted in during the day.

## What you will notice

By Sunday evening the room will look emptier than you expected. That is the point. Spend a few nights in it before adding anything else. Most people find they miss very little, and that the first thing they notice is not the new linen or the curtains, but the quiet.
