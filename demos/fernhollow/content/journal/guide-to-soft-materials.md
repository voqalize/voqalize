We spend a lot of time choosing the shape of a sofa and very little thinking about what it is covered in, even though the fabric is the part we actually touch. The same goes for cushions, throws and rugs. Soft materials set the temperature of a room, both literally and in mood. They decide whether a space feels crisp or cosy, formal or relaxed, and they decide how it will look after five years of real life.

Four fabrics do most of the work in a calm, natural home: linen, boucle, wool and cotton. Each has a character, a set of strengths and a few habits worth knowing.

## Linen: relaxed and breathable

Linen is woven from flax, and it is the fabric that makes a room look like summer even in winter. It has a dry, slightly cool hand, a natural slub in the weave and a matte surface that catches light softly. Its most famous trait is the crease. Linen wrinkles, and the best thing to do is enjoy it: the relaxed rumple is part of the look.

Linen breathes well, so it is comfortable to sit on in warm weather, and it tends to soften with use and washing rather than wearing thin. Stonewashed linen arrives already softened, with that lived-in look from the start.

## Boucle: texture you can see from across the room

Boucle takes its name from the French for "curl". The yarn is looped, so the finished fabric has a nubbly, pebbled surface that looks soft and invites touch. It photographs beautifully and adds depth to a room built from plain neutrals, because a single colour in boucle never looks flat.

Boucle shines on curved, sculptural pieces where its texture follows the shape. Because the surface is looped, keep it away from pets with sharp claws and from rough fabrics or velcro that might catch it.

## Wool: warmth and resilience

Wool is the workhorse of soft materials. The fibres have a natural crimp that gives them spring, so wool cushions underfoot and recovers from pressure. It is warm in the hand and in the eye, and it is the classic choice for rugs that see daily traffic.

When you choose a wool rug, look at the construction as well as the fibre. Hand-knotted rugs are made knot by knot, which gives them density and character; flatweaves are thinner, lighter and easier to move. Expect a new wool rug to shed a little for its first weeks; regular, gentle vacuuming settles it.

## Cotton: the easy everyday

Cotton is soft, familiar and versatile. It takes dye well, it is easy to wash, and it comes in every weight from fine percale to chunky woven rugs. It is the natural choice for anything that needs frequent laundering, and for rooms where children and pets make easy care a priority.

## Layering them together

The most comfortable rooms rarely use one fabric alone. They layer two or three, varying texture while keeping colour quiet. A few principles help:

- **Pair a smooth fabric with a textured one.** Linen beside boucle, cotton beside wool. Contrast in texture is what makes a neutral room interesting.
- **Keep the palette narrow when the textures are varied.** Oat, sand, stone and cream in four different fabrics looks rich. Four textures in four strong colours looks busy.
- **Use throws as the movable layer.** A throw changes a room with the season: linen in summer, something heavier when the evenings draw in.
- **Put the hardest-wearing fabric where use is heaviest.** Wool on the floor of a hallway, boucle on the reading chair, linen on the sofa where its creases are welcome.

## Caring for soft materials

Fabrics last longest when they are cared for little and often. Plump and rotate cushions weekly so they wear evenly. Vacuum upholstery and rugs with a soft brush attachment. Blot spills straight away with a clean, dry cloth, working from the edge inward, and never rub. Keep everything out of strong, direct sunlight where you can, since all natural fibres fade over time. Always check a product's own care label before washing, and see our care guide for more on each material.

Choose fabrics for the way you live, not just the way they look in a picture, and they will reward you by looking better each year.
