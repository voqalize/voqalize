## Explora Conservation in brief (/explora-conservation-reserves/)
- Explora is building "fully protected, financially self-sustaining Conservation Reserves in South America", with Explora-only Explorations and membership, toward a vision "to ultimately safeguard one million hectares — nearly 2.5 million acres". The older /conservation-reserves/ page says "a positive impact on one million hectares over the next 10 years".
- Model: a venture that "joins the virtues of business enterprise and non-profit commitment", because "philanthropy often fades in the long run".
- **Two Explora reserves**: Torres del Paine (Patagonia, the flagship) and Puritama (Atacama). The El Chaltén lodge sits in the 14,000-acre **Los Huemules Conservation Reserve**, which /el-chalten-hotel/ calls "our long-standing conservation partners in the region" (not an Explora reserve).
- Other projects: educational alliances with the Terevaka Foundation, conservation plans for Puritama and the UNESCO Torres del Paine Biosphere Reserve, and the **Toki Pilot Forest** in Rapa Nui. A 2024 Impact Report is linked (issuu.com).
- Partners: The Nature Conservancy, Rewilding Chile, Terevaka Foundation, ECOAN (the volunteering page also names Microsoft for Torres del Paine). Explora is a **Certified B Corporation** and **Launching Member of the Glasgow Declaration**; 2018 National Environment Award for the Puritama reserve.
- Four pillars: Community & Collaboration; Guided by Science (camera traps, indicators); Education Across All Levels; Excellence in Stewardship (own park rangers, trails, Explorations).
- Explora's care for these places "has been part of Explora's DNA since its inception in 1993".

## Torres del Paine Conservation Reserve (Patagonia, Chile)
- Northeast of Torres del Paine National Park, "on the edge" of it but not inside; within the UNESCO Torres del Paine Biosphere Reserve; between the Paine Horns and the Sierra Baguales; Jara Lagoon at its heart; the Zamora and Las Chinas rivers cross it. One post says it is "just 1.3 km from Laguna Azul".
- Former estancias (ranches) of gauchos; Explora began studying it in **2019**, created with The Nature Conservancy, legally protected in perpetuity by a DRC (derecho real de conservación, "Right of Conservation").
- Restoration: livestock removed, fences opened, clean-ups; "hundreds of guanacos and other native species have returned"; pumas re-established; regenerative grazing project; **reforestation began in 2023**, plus visitor trails with educational signage; "now open to the public".
- Trails: 22 miles (35 km) so far, planned 62 miles (100 km) in total; GPS route linked on suda.io.
- Highlights on /new-torres-del-paine-conservation-reserve/: 130+ plant species (over 96% native), 140+ bird species, 22 mammal species, 14,800 acres (6,000 ha), 40% forest.
- **Explorations** there: horseback rides with gauchos on Explora's own horses (bred from Chilean, English and Arabian lines), hikes to the Las Chinas waterfall, asados at the historic quincho. Guests at both Torres del Paine lodges can explore it; the new 10-room lodge sits inside it (topic `torres-del-paine-reserve`).
- **Membership**: a conservation community "limited to just 100 nature-loving members"; benefits include unlimited Explorations, stays at the Lodge and a four-bedroom Guest House, privileged travel at Explora lodges, the quincho for private gatherings, horse stabling, and home ownership on homesites ("Explora's first-ever homesites"). Enquiries via conservationreserves.org ("Contact our Conservation Reserve Team", /become-a-member). No prices are given.
- **Felines**: camera traps; in 2022 a Geoffroy's cat was recorded in Torres del Paine; puma, Geoffroy's cat and the rare Colocolo (pampas) cat are recorded in Patagonia.
- **Regenerative grazing** (Nov 2022 post): announced October 2022; Explora has 30 horses in the reserve; strategic grazing began January 2022; advised by José Manuel Gortazar; working toward Savory Institute certification; 70 local people trained by October 2022. Quotes from Nicolás Vial (Conservation Reserves Manager) and Romina Da Pieve.

### Volunteering (/torres-del-paine-conservation-reserve-volunteering/)
Join the park rangers living in the reserve. Tasks: removing old fences and trail restoration; monitoring wildlife and flora including camera traps; ecological restoration and regenerative grazing; maintenance. Requirements: good physical and emotional condition, teamwork, willingness to learn or improve Spanish, personal accident insurance, **at least 21 years old**. Duration: **minimum 1 month**, extendable. "Apply here" link on the page.

## Puritama Conservation Reserve (Atacama, Chile)
- **60 km north of San Pedro de Atacama**, along the Puritama River from the Altiplano into the Atacama Desert, including the **Puritama hot springs**; between 3,700 and 4,200 meters above sea level.
- Highlights (/new-puritama/): 8 hot-spring pools at 28° to 31° C; 83 flora species; 87 fauna species; Andean cat monitored by Explora since 2010.
- Wildlife: Andean cat, puma, pampas (Colocolo) cat, foxes, vizcachas, guanacos, nearly 30 bird species, lizards and frogs. In 2022 cameras recorded a mother puma with her cub. Monitoring with the Alianza Gato Andino (AGA). The Andean cat page says about 1,400 Andean cats are estimated to exist.
- Visitors buy tickets for hot-spring experiences (termasdepuritama.cl); Explora guests "go deeper with breathtaking hikes into lush canyons and swims in private hot springs".
- Culture: Likan Antai (Atacama people) myths of Licancabur and Kimal; Licancabur's winter-solstice shadow "stretches more than 100 kilometers".

## Where the site disagrees with itself
- **Torres del Paine reserve size**: 15,000 acres (home, /torres-del-paine-conservation-reserve-2025/ ownership text, /lodge/torres-del-paine-lodge/); 14,800 acres (hub, /new-…, volunteering); "nearly 15,000 acres (6,000 hectares)" (new lodge pages); "over 6000 hectare" (Biodiversity tab). The El Chaltén page's 14,000 acres is Los Huemules, a different reserve.
- **Share kept for conservation**: home says 95%; /torres-del-paine-conservation-reserve-2025/ says 97% (3% for tourism, research, education).
- **Species counts**: 2019 study "around 130 types of plants, 50 types of birds and 15 species of mammals" (2025 page) or 131 flora / 50 birds / 15 mammals (2022 post); stat cards "131 Flora Species… 40 Fauna Species… 33 types of birds" (2025 page); "Over a hundred resident bird species" (same page, tab); "130+ plant species, 140+ bird species, 22 mammal species" (/new-…).
- **Forest**: 32% forest, 19% native (2025 page) vs 40% forest (/new-…); "80 percent native vegetation" (/torres-del-paine-explorations-page/) vs "over 96 percent are native" (/new-…).
- **Estancias**: "two Patagonian Estancias" (2025 page, 2022 felines post) vs "three important cattle ranches" (2022 gauchos post) and "the three former estancias" (Florence exploration).
- **Volunteer age**: "at least 21" (requirements) vs "between 21 and 35 years old" (intro).
- **Puritama size**: 7,000 ha / 17,300 acres (hub, /new-puritama/, 2022 post) vs 6,440 ha (/new-puritama/ stat card) vs 6,000 ha (Andean cat page). **Cameras**: 13 (Andean cat page) vs 20 (2022 post).
- **Year count**: "Thirty years ago" and "World Leading Expedition Company 2019" (old /conservation-reserves/ page) vs "more than 30 years" and the 2025 award elsewhere.
